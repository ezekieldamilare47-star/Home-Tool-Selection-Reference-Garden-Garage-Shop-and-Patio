This repository-style reference condenses buying and maintenance guidance for home, yard, garage and patio tools into scannable tables. The primary source is [Practical Backyard](https://practicalbackyard.com/), the personal website of homeowner Eddie van Aken, which organizes its content into three main areas: Yard & Garden Tools, Garage Tools (Essential Hand Tools, Essential Electric Tools, Shop & Garage Equipment) and Backyard Living (patio furniture, patio heaters, hot tubs and outdoor accessories).

## Evaluation Criteria

Every review starts with: "Would I consider buying this myself?" The eight criteria below come from its [product review process](https://practicalbackyard.com/how-i-test-and-review-products/).

| Criterion | What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| How well it does the job | Performance on the actual task you bought it for | Core performance outweighs feature lists |
| Ease of use | Setup, controls, weight, how it feels in the hand | Tools that are awkward to use end up sitting on a shelf |
| Build quality | Materials, joints, fasteners, overall solidity | Determines whether the tool survives regular use |
| Useful features | Features that make the job easier, not just longer spec sheets | Extra features only add value if you will use them |
| Price and value | Cost relative to what you get and how often you will use it | The most expensive option is not automatically the best |
| Reliability | Consistent performance over time, common failure points | A tool that fails mid-project costs time and money |
| Maintenance | Cleaning, sharpening, lubrication, replaceable parts | Low-maintenance tools last longer with less effort |
| What other owners say | Patterns across owner reviews and common complaints | Long-term owner feedback reveals problems a spec sheet hides |

## Garden Tool Guide Reference

The source's garden tool guide groups yard and garden equipment into eight categories. Its core point for beginners: "You don't need dozens of tools to get started."

| Category | Tools | Beginner kit? |
|---|---|---|
| Cutting and pruning | Pruning shears, loppers, pruning saws | Yes (pruning shears) |
| Digging and planting | Shovel or spade, garden fork, hand trowel | Yes (all three) |
| Soil prep and lawn care | Rakes, hand cultivators, lawn aerators | No |
| Lawn maintenance | String trimmers, edgers, leaf blowers | No |
| Watering | Hoses, sprinklers, nozzles, watering cans | Yes (hose) |
| Composting | Compost tumblers, bins | No |
| Moving materials | Wheelbarrows, garden carts | No |
| Gloves and protective gear | Gloves, kneelers, sleeves | Yes (gloves) |

**Six-item starter kit:** pruning shears, shovel or spade, garden fork, hand trowel, hose, gloves.

**Selection specs to look for:** hardened steel, comfortable ergonomic handles, good balance, replaceable or sharpenable parts, and weather resistance. For the full breakdown, see the [garden tools buying guide](https://practicalbackyard.com/garden-tools/).

## Garage Tools Guide Reference

The garage tools guide notes: "Most of the time, we use hand tools for our projects."

| Type | Use | Buying tip |
|---|---|---|
| Essential hand tools | Everyday repairs, assembly and most DIY projects | "Durable metals and solid construction matter"; buy these first |
| Essential electric tools | Drilling, cutting and driving jobs that would be slow by hand | Power tools "can make our lives as DIYers a lot easier," but match them to projects you actually do |
| Bench vise | "Holds metal and wooden materials securely in place" while you cut, file or drill | Confirm you have bench space and a solid mounting surface first |

**Decision factors:** how often you will use the tool, which projects you take on, and how much space you have. "Comfortable handles reduce fatigue," so test grip where possible. Plan storage before buying, and avoid tools you don't need.

## Shop and Garage Equipment Guides Reference

The shop and garage equipment guides cover three larger purchases. The stated philosophy: "I don't believe in buying every tool on the market." The goal is to "build a practical garage without buying tools you may never use."

| Equipment | Common uses | Key decision |
|---|---|---|
| Air compressor | Inflating tires, powering air tools, cleaning hard-to-reach areas, winterizing a sprinkler system | Which of these jobs you will actually perform regularly |
| Pressure washer | Driveways, patios, siding, vehicles, outdoor furniture | Choosing the right PSI for your surfaces, plus maintenance needs and accessories such as surface cleaners |
| Garage vacuum | Sawdust, wood chips, drywall dust, wet spills | Whether you need wet and dry capability; "A good garage vacuum is built to handle the dirt and debris that regular household vacuums aren't designed for" |

For PSI sizing, see the source article "What PSI Pressure Washer Do I Need?"

## Patio Accessories Guide Reference

The patio accessories guide's weather test: buy things that "still look great after it gets rained, snowed, or hailed on."

| Accessory | Buying check |
|---|---|
| Patio furniture and seating | Sit in it before buying; look for "removable and replaceable covers" and machine-washable cushion covers |
| Patio heaters | Choose among electric, gas and infrared; consider tabletop or umbrella-mounted styles for small spaces |
| Patio umbrellas | Plan your shade around how and when you use the space |
| Fire pits, fire tables, outdoor lighting | Choose by lifestyle and how you entertain |
| Storage boxes | Size for cushions and covers you need to protect |
| Planters, rugs, side tables, TV covers, inflatable hot tubs, social grilling tables | Confirm they hold up to rain, snow and hail |

**Budget rule:** "Set a budget in advance and stick to it." If you are not in a hurry, buy on sale. Online, favor detailed reviews and flexible returns.

## Maintenance Checklist

- [ ] Clean garden tools after each use
- [ ] Sharpen pruning blades regularly
- [ ] Oil moving parts on shears, loppers and hinged tools
- [ ] "Dry tools before storing them to prevent rust"
- [ ] Keep garage tools clean and lubricated
- [ ] Wash removable patio cushion covers

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| "Buying very cheap tools that wear out quickly" | Prioritize hardened steel, solid construction and replaceable parts |
| Buying dozens of garden tools up front | Start with the six-item beginner kit and add as needs appear |
| Buying shop equipment for jobs you rarely do | Match air compressors, pressure washers and vacuums to real tasks |
| Picking pressure washer PSI at random | Size PSI to the surfaces you clean most |
| Ignoring weather on patio purchases | Choose pieces built for rain, snow and hail |

## Source and Methodology

Practical Backyard was started back in 2010 by Eddie van Aken, a homeowner based in Elkton, Kentucky, who has "spent years working around my own home, garage, yard, garden, and patio." In his words, it "isn't a big review website with a team of writers testing hundreds of products. It's my personal website." The stated aim is to "give you the information I would want if I were getting ready to spend my own money."

| Method | What it involves | How it is labeled |
|---|---|---|
| Hands-on use | Feel, setup, whether it makes the job easier, little annoyances | Personal experience is labeled as such |
| Research | Specs, manufacturer info, owner experiences, warranties, common complaints and multiple review sources, looking for patterns | No claim of use for products not personally used |

Reviews cover good and bad points, and affiliate commissions do not change the opinion. "No product is perfect," and no single tool is claimed to suit everyone.

## FAQ

**What belongs in a beginner garden tool guide kit?**
Pruning shears, a shovel or spade, a garden fork, a hand trowel, a hose and gloves.

**Which garage tools guide category should I buy from first?**
Hand tools. They handle most projects; add electric tools and a bench vise as your work demands.

**What do the shop and garage equipment guides recommend checking on a pressure washer?**
The PSI rating for your surfaces, ongoing maintenance and useful accessories like surface cleaners.

**What is the main rule in a patio accessories guide?**
Buy pieces that survive the weather, set a budget in advance, and favor replaceable, washable covers.
